# Working with AI

## It is part of software development now

You will meet AI in your editor, your browser and other developers' habits.

This course does not ban it, and does not require it.

<!--
Introduce this as a shared agreement, not a warning or a test. Many learners are already using AI; say that you expect it and want to talk about it openly. Invite questions at any point.

Do not ask learners to confess or declare their usage. There is nothing to defend.
-->

---

# Three routes, all legitimate

- **Without AI** - documentation, error messages, Developer Tools, other people
- **With AI to learn and check** - explain, review, quiz, debug
- **With AI to build** - you direct it, it drafts, you review

You can change route from week to week.

<!--
No route is the "proper" one. Every outcome in this course is achievable on the first, and no paid AI tool is required.

Expect learners to arrive with a mix of tools and experience. Stay tool-neutral: do not recommend a product. Acknowledge that free tiers and paid tools can feel very different, and that the course tasks are deliberately small enough to work either way.

Do not present the third route as riskier or more advanced than the first. Do not present the first as the more virtuous.
-->

---

# Whichever route, you are the developer

- You decide what to build
- You decide what to keep
- You can read it, explain it, test it and change it

If you cannot, you are not finished with it yet.

<!--
This is the one fixed point. It applies equally with and without AI. Assessment in Week 8 is about whether learners can explain and defend their work, not how it was produced.
-->

---

# Keep some things out

Do not paste into an AI service:

- Passwords, tokens or private keys
- Personal or confidential information
- Private client material

## AI cannot give you permission to reuse Directors Notes images, stills, video, logos or article text.

<!--
Your repository, README and Project board may be public, so this applies from today.

Own-project learners: you still need to own, or have permission to use, anything you publish.

Publicly visible does not automatically mean reusable.
-->

---

# A shared agreement

During this bootcamp I will:

- Choose how I use AI, and be honest with myself about it
- Stay the developer: I decide, review and own my project
- Be able to explain what I keep
- Check what AI tells me
- Protect private and unlicensed material

<!--
Invite discussion or objections before treating this as agreed. It is a list of habits for the room, not a document to sign.

Next week, once learners have something to build, the practice slides cover how to work well with AI, how to work well without it, and how to check what you keep.
-->

---

# My goal isn't to build software with AI.

# My goal is to become a software developer who knows how to use AI well.

<!--
Return to this statement throughout the course, especially in Week 8 reflection.
-->

# Every brief has the same shape

- **Intended visitor** - who it should help
